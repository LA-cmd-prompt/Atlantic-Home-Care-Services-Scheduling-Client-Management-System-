# Atlantic-Home-Care-Services-Scheduling-Client-Management-System-

**Client Type:** Home Care Agency (Healthcare)  
**Role:** Business Analyst (Process & Requirements Lead)  
**Focus:** Collaborative requirements gathering, feasible documentation, and stakeholder engagement


## Engagement Timeline & Approach

### Month 1: Discovery & Current State Analysis
I started by building a clear picture of how the agency currently operated. I reviewed:
- Existing scheduling spreadsheets (3 months of data)
- Incident and complaint logs
- Staff shift records and overtime reports
- Client feedback forms

I also reviewed the **company organogram** to understand reporting lines and who had formal versus informal influence. This helped me identify both obvious and hidden stakeholders.

From this review, I established baseline metrics: the agency was experiencing **~35 missed or rescheduled visits per month**, and schedulers were spending an average of **4 hours daily** on manual coordination.

### Month 2: Stakeholder Analysis & Collaborative Engagement
Using the organogram and initial findings, I created a **Stakeholder Power/Interest Matrix**. This helped me understand who had high influence and high interest (scheduling managers and clinical leads) versus those who needed to be kept informed (field nurses and caregivers).

Because many frontline staff struggled to articulate their needs in traditional meetings, I ran collaborative workshops using silent sticky-note brainstorming and “Day in the Life” walkthroughs. These techniques removed the pressure of speaking in front of others and helped quieter voices contribute comfortably. The sessions turned abstract frustrations into visible process issues that everyone could see and discuss.

### Month 3: Requirements Documentation, Validation & Traceability
I translated the workshop outcomes into structured requirements, applying a **developer lens** from the beginning. Every requirement included clear acceptance criteria (Given-When-Then format), realistic performance expectations, and integration considerations.

I also created a **Requirements Traceability Matrix** and facilitated walkthrough sessions with both business and technical stakeholders to validate the requirements and surface feasibility issues early.

---

## Key Results & Impact

- **42% reduction** in scheduling conflicts within the first 3 months of going live
- **35% decrease** in time spent on daily scheduling (from ~4 hours to under 2.5 hours)
- **28% improvement** in caregiver utilization rate
- **19% reduction** in missed or rescheduled visits
- **Significantly higher** staff satisfaction with the new process (qualitative feedback from 87% of schedulers and nurses)


### Trade-offs Considered
- **Speed vs Quality**: We could have moved faster by skipping detailed acceptance criteria, but I recommended investing the time upfront. This trade-off paid off as very little rework was needed during implementation.
- **Scope vs Budget**: The agency wanted several nice-to-have features (advanced reporting, mobile app for clients). After reviewing effort versus value, I recommended a phased approach, starting with core scheduling functionality first.
- **Change Management**: Moving from spreadsheets to a system represented a big shift for some staff. I recommended keeping the initial rollout simple to reduce resistance.

### Recommendations
I recommended the following:

1. Proceed with a digital scheduling system focused on real-time availability and conflict detection.
2. Prioritize mobile access for caregivers in the first release.
3. Implement a 3-month pilot with one scheduling team before full rollout.
4. Include a simple feedback loop so schedulers and nurses could suggest improvements post-implementation.

These recommendations were accepted, and the project moved forward with strong cross-functional support.

These results were achieved by focusing on **collaborative workshops** and writing **feasible, well-defined requirements** from the start.
